I've been doubting the project I'm building right now, and I want to write that down honestly instead of waiting until I have something polished to say.

I'm keeping the details of the project to myself while it's still being built. What it does isn't the point of this post. The doubt is. The code was never the hard part. The questions around it are.

## The Questions I Can't Stop Asking

The same questions keep coming back, and I can't seem to stop asking them:

- Will this project actually work?
- If it works, do I need to register it as its own business entity?
- Will anyone really use it?
- How do I find partners who are willing to be part of it?
- What do I charge? I don't know what the rates are in this field.

I don't have an answer to any of them yet. I want to be clear about that, because a post about doubt that ends with a tidy answer wouldn't be honest.

## Where My Experience Ends

My experience is in building software. I can work on it one task at a time, and I can tell when a task is done.

It ends there. The questions above are about a business: whether a company should exist around this, who would use it, who would partner with me, and what it should cost. I have no experience with any of it, and I don't know how rates get decided.

A project like this also depends on people other than me. It needs users, and it needs partners who are willing to take part. I haven't worked out how to find my first users or my first partners.

## Building a Product vs. Building a Business

This is the realization I keep landing on: building a product and building a business are two separate things. I think I had been treating them as one, as if finishing the product would also take care of the business. It doesn't.

- Building a product means making the software work.
- Building a business means everything around it: whether anyone wants it, who partners with me, what it costs, and whether it needs to be registered as a company.

I can be fairly far along on one and still be at the very beginning of the other. That's why adding more features doesn't settle my doubts. The doubts aren't about the software.

## When Doubt Gets in the Way

I feel like I'm building something on an idea I assumed would work, and that feeling is what prevents me from moving forward.

Doubt is not a verdict. It doesn't mean the idea is wrong, and it doesn't mean it is right. It means I'm missing information.

## Why I'm Writing This

I'm not writing this to announce a decision or a lesson, because I don't have one yet. I'm writing it because one large doubt (“will this work?”) is hard to look at, and a list of specific questions is easier to look at. Some are about the market, some about registration, some about pricing, and some about people. They probably have different answers and different people to ask.
